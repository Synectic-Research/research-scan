## 1. The Semblance of Success in Nudging Consumers to Pay Down Credit Card Debt

Benedict Guttman‐Kenney, Paul Adams, Stefan Hunt, David Laibson et al. · 2023 · National Bureau of Economic Research · rct · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w31926>

**Key finding.** A field experiment shrouding the credit-card minimum-payment autopay option reduced minimum-only payers by 23% after six months but did not significantly reduce credit card debt, in part because it also reduced autopay enrollment and increased missed payments among liquidity-constrained cardholders.

**Why it made the cut.** contradicting · selected by score · strongest on C1 default effect size (3/3). Directly tests a consumer financial nudge/default change with liquidity-constrained users and shows enrollment change decoupled from the real outcome — closest empirical analog to the round-up decision.

**Why it matters here.** Shows that changing enrollment defaults for a liquid, self-directed consumer financial product can shift nominal enrollment behavior without changing the underlying financial outcome, and can even backfire via missed payments among liquidity-constrained users — directly bears on whether opt-out enrollment in round-up savings will produce real savings or just nominal sign-ups.

**Method.** Field experiment on credit card autopay enrollment with six-month follow-up.

**Limitations.**

- about credit card debt repayment, not savings accumulation
- abstract gives no effect size on debt itself, only on payment behavior

## 2. Framing the Default Option Right

Luc Meunier, Yashar Bashirzadeh, Sima Ohadi · 2024 · Journal of Behavioral Decision Making · experimental · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.2395>

**Key finding.** In experiments including a representative five-country European sample (n=4207), default options for risky-asset allocation produce medium-to-large effects on risk-taking, but implementing a default nudge (especially one the client would refuse) lowers the client's rating of the wealth manager's advice, with framing of the default question also shifting allocations.

**Why it made the cut.** design-changing · selected by score · strongest on C1 default effect size (3/3). European, consumer-facing financial-decision experiment directly measuring when a default is perceived as manipulative, exactly the reputational risk question the brief raises.

**Why it matters here.** Directly tests when a financial default reads as manipulative versus helpful in a European consumer-facing advisory context, showing defaults can damage trust — this is the exact dark-pattern/reputational risk the brief flags for our EU product, and cautions against a one-size-fits-all preset multiplier.

**Method.** Three experiments, including a large representative-sample experiment across five European countries testing default nudges and framing in investment decisions.

**Limitations.**

- investment/wealth management context rather than a savings round-up feature
- no follow-up beyond the initial decision, so persistence unmeasured

## 3. Dark defaults: How choice architecture steers political campaign donations

Nathaniel A. Posner, A. M. Simonov, Kellen Mrkva, Eric J. Johnson · 2023 · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · observational · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2218385120>

**Key finding.** Pre-checked recurring-donation defaults on political campaign websites raised donations by over $43 million but also increased refund requests by almost $3 million, with the largest effects on smaller, less-experienced donors who did not compensate by changing donation amounts.

**Why it made the cut.** closely-related · selected by score · strongest on C1 default effect size (3/3). Directly tests default effects on amount and heterogeneity in a self-directed consumer digital-payment setting close to the brief's own concerns.

**Why it matters here.** A near-perfect consumer-digital analogue to a pre-checked enrollment default: it shows a default can raise nominal sign-ups while generating complaints/refunds and disproportionately affecting inexperienced users, mirroring the support team's exact worry about the round-up feature.

**Method.** Staggered difference-in-differences design across multiple 2020 US campaign websites that added pre-checked recurring-donation boxes at different times.

**Limitations.**

- political donations rather than personal savings
- refund/complaint outcome is a proxy for disengagement, not a direct engagement or withdrawal measure

## 4. Smaller than We Thought? The Effect of Automatic Savings Policies

James J. Choi, David Laibson, Jordan Cammarota, Richard Lombardo et al. · 2024 · National Bureau of Economic Research · observational · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w32828>

**Key finding.** Medium- and long-run dynamics undermine the measured effect of automatic enrollment and default contribution auto-escalation; steady-state saving rates rise only 0.6% of income from automatic enrollment and 0.3% from default auto-escalation, and only 40% of those with an auto-escalation default escalate on their first date.

**Why it made the cut.** contradicting · selected by score · strongest on C1 default effect size (3/3). Contradicts the premise that defaults reliably raise saving amounts long-term, which the brief asked us to look for.

**Why it matters here.** The clearest statement of our support team's objection: the enrolment lift is real at signup and much smaller later — sets the ceiling our default-design decision should be measured against.

**Method.** Longitudinal analysis of nine 401(k) plans incorporating job separation, balance withdrawal, and auto-escalation opt-out.

**Limitations.**

- retirement plans rather than liquid consumer savings
- abstract gives 401(k)-specific figures that may not map onto round-up savings

## 6. Sending Out an SMS: Automatic Enrollment Experiments for Overdraft Alerts

Michael D. Grubb, Darragh Kelly, Jeroen Nieboer, Matthew Osborne et al. · 2024 · The Journal of Finance · rct · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13404>

**Key finding.** At-scale UK bank field experiments show automatic enrollment into just-in-time overdraft alerts cuts unarranged overdraft charges 17-19% and arranged overdraft charges 4-8%, worth an estimated £170-240 million annually, though alerts still leave more than half of avoidable overdrafts unaddressed.

**Why it made the cut.** design-changing · selected by score · strongest on C1 default effect size (3/3). The closest available consumer-banking automatic-enrollment evidence with real effect sizes, directly bearing on the entry decision.

**Why it matters here.** This is a consumer banking automatic-enrollment result, not a pension analogy: it shows defaults can move real financial behavior at scale in exactly our setting, while also showing a persistent gap between potential and realized benefit that supports the support team's disengagement concern.

**Method.** Large-scale randomized field experiments run by major UK retail banks on live customer accounts.

**Limitations.**

- measures charge avoidance, not savings amounts or a round-up product specifically
- no explicit 6-12 month retention/withdrawal analysis

## 8. When Nudges Backfire: The Effect of Social Norms, Framing Effects, and Default Options on the Pension Saving Decisions in China

Yuming Dong, Zhaoyue Wang, Zhuting He · 2025 · Advances in Economics, Management and Political Sciences · rct · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.54254/2754-1169/2025.bl30018>

**Key finding.** In a 220-participant RCT on voluntary pension saving in China, all tested nudges including default options significantly reduced willingness to contribute relative to control, contrary to prevailing international evidence, with the largest backfire from positive-narrative and default nudges.

**Why it made the cut.** contradicting · selected by score · strongest on C1 default effect size (3/3). Directly requested by the brief: a study contradicting the assumption that defaults reliably raise saving, with distributional detail on who is helped or hurt.

**Why it matters here.** A direct empirical contradiction of the 'defaults always help' premise the brief asks us to test, in a self-directed voluntary-savings decision context with reported heterogeneity by income, education, and gender.

**Method.** Randomized controlled trial via online survey testing social-norm, framing, default, and narrative nudges on voluntary pension contribution decisions.

**Limitations.**

- voluntary pension saving in China, cultural/institutional context may differ from EU consumer banking
- online survey stated intentions rather than live product enrollment or actual money

## 10. Side Effects of Nudging: Evidence from a Randomized Intervention in the Credit Card Market

Paolina C. Medina · 2020 · Review of Financial Studies · rct · overall 3/3

<https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa108>

**Key finding.** A field experiment with a financial-management platform found payment reminders cut late-payment fees by 14% overall but raised overdraft fees by 9%, concentrated in users with an overdraft history, who saw a net 5% increase in total fees while everyone else saved 15%.

**Why it made the cut.** contradicting · selected by foundational · strongest on C4 consumer self-directed setting (3/3). Best available evidence in a consumer financial self-directed setting for the brief's explicit worry about who is helped versus hurt by nudge-style interventions.

**Why it matters here.** Directly evidences the support team's concern that a well-intentioned nudge can help most users on average while actively harming a vulnerable subgroup — exactly the heterogeneity risk the brief says we must check before defaulting everyone into round-up savings.

**Method.** Randomized field experiment on a consumer financial-management platform in Brazil comparing reminder vs. no-reminder groups, tracking downstream fee outcomes across products.

**Limitations.**

- studies payment reminders, not enrollment defaults or preset contribution levels
- credit card/overdraft context rather than round-up savings
- no long-horizon (6-12 month) persistence data reported in abstract
